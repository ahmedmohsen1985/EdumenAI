# EXERCISE 4 RUBRIC, TONES, TYPES AND STANDARDISATION ANCHORS
# Oxford AQA IGCSE ESL Exercise 4: Article Writing (23 marks)

## Mark Allocation
- **Content & Communication**: 8 marks
- **Language**: 15 marks
- **Total**: 23 marks

---

## CONTENT & COMMUNICATION (8 marks)

Award marks holistically based on how well the article:
- addresses the task and audience;
- develops ideas with relevant detail and examples;
- organises information clearly;
- uses an appropriate and consistent tone.

### Content & Communication Levels
- **7-8 marks**: Relevant, well-developed ideas; clear sense of audience; engaging and coherent throughout.
- **5-6 marks**: Relevant ideas with some development; generally clear organisation; mostly appropriate tone.
- **3-4 marks**: Some relevant ideas but limited development; organisation may be uneven; tone not always appropriate.
- **1-2 marks**: Few relevant ideas; little development or organisation; tone often inappropriate.
- **0 marks**: No relevant content or completely off-task.

---

## LANGUAGE (15 marks)

### Level 5: 13-15 marks
- Accurate and varied sentence structures.
- Wide range of vocabulary used precisely.
- Errors are rare and do not affect communication.
- Effective use of paragraphs and linking words.

### Level 4: 10-12 marks
- Generally accurate sentence structures with some variety.
- Adequate range of vocabulary, mostly used appropriately.
- Errors are minor and rarely impede understanding.
- Paragraphing and linking are mostly effective.

### Level 3: 7-9 marks
- Simple and some complex sentence structures; some errors.
- Vocabulary is adequate but may be repetitive or imprecise.
- Errors sometimes affect meaning.
- Some attempt at paragraphing and linking.

### Level 2: 4-6 marks
- Mainly simple sentences; frequent errors.
- Limited vocabulary; some words used incorrectly.
- Errors often affect meaning or clarity.
- Little evidence of paragraphing or linking.

### Level 1: 1-3 marks
- Very simple or broken sentences; many errors.
- Very limited vocabulary; meaning frequently unclear.
- Communication is seriously impaired.

### Level 0: 0 marks
- No awardable language.

---

## ARTICLE TONES

1. **Formal**: Third-person, objective, no contractions, suitable for school magazines or official publications.
2. **Semi-formal**: Directly addresses the reader using "you", friendly but polite, suitable for general magazine articles.
3. **Informal**: Personal experience, first-person, conversational, suitable for blogs or teen magazines.

The tone must match the task. Penalise inconsistent tone only if it damages communication or audience awareness.

---

## ARTICLE TYPES

1. **Discursive**: Presents different sides of an issue; balanced; ends with a clear conclusion.
2. **Argumentative**: Takes a clear position and supports it with reasons and evidence.
3. **Persuasive**: Aims to convince the reader; uses engaging openings, rhetorical questions, and direct address.

---

## PILAR FRAMEWORK FOR ARTICLE WRITING

Each strong article should show evidence of:
- **P**urpose: Why is the article being written? Clear from the start.
- **I**nterest: Does the opening hook the reader?
- **L**inkage: Are ideas connected with appropriate linking words?
- **A**udience: Is the reader directly or indirectly addressed?
- **R**ange: Is there variety in vocabulary and sentence structure?

Use PILAR to guide content feedback, not to calculate marks mechanically.

## HOW TO USE THE ANCHORS
1. Read the student's response.
2. Ask which anchor it MOST CLOSELY resembles in quality.
3. Award the same band as that anchor, then adjust ±1 within the band based on whether the student's response is slightly stronger or weaker than the anchor.
4. Do NOT default to middle bands. If the response resembles Anchor Q4-C, award high. If it resembles Anchor Q4-B, award low.
5. Examiners forgive minor blemishes when the overall weight of accurate writing is strong. Anchor Q4-C scored 15/15 with visible spelling errors. Match that generosity.
